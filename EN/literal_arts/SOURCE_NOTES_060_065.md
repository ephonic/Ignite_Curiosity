# Source notes: liberal-arts fragments 060–063

Targeted observations during translation, not a comprehensive independent fact-check. Philosophical positions and source-era examples remain attributed arguments, not new empirical, clinical or legal guidance. Root owns assembly and full-PDF validation.

## 060 — What the world is made of

- The bicycle workshop and final copying scenario are imagined. A welded/cracked frame is not endorsed as safe to ride; disassembling a person described as harmless is the source's hypothetical wording, not established technology or safety.
- Physicalism, dualism and idealism are introductory positions, not settled consciousness findings. Causal closure, thoughts' indivisibility, all apparent mysteries eventually explained, and idealism's priority of perception remain their presented arguments. Berkeley's table argument and Wang Yangming's account are not identical theories. The latter and the Zhuangzi/Diamond Sutra passages are newly translated from supplied Chinese; original French Descartes text retained.
- Source introduces Heraclitus as looking east and near Plutarch's period, although he was Greek and lived roughly six centuries earlier. Hobbes came over a millennium after Plutarch, not merely a few centuries, and Locke's modern account is rhetorically called a two-thousand-year preview. Final 2,400-year Athenian dating differs from the earlier two-thousand-year framing. These chronology tensions are preserved.
- Melting ice conserves its material, contrary to the source's claim this example uses continuity rather than material identity. Sand-pile, musical-work and organization criteria are illustrative and contested, not fixed metaphysical laws. Early claims about total bodily turnover are later explicitly corrected by the chapter itself; that correction is preserved.
- Whitehead's process ontology, four-dimensional spacetime and temporal-part theories are related pedagogical comparisons, not equivalent theories or a scientific proof of perdurantism. Flame/stone burning is expressly metaphorical for stone. Digital bits can be preserved as information even when storage/electrons change; no-original-bits language is source shorthand. AI-copy personhood is presented as an unanswered question, not an assertion of consciousness or rights.

## 061 — Do humans have free will?

- The robbery trial is imagined, not a reported legal case. The ending attributes to the victim's mother a question earlier spoken by the prosecutor; this inconsistency is preserved. Later compatibility discussion introduces planning and deliberation not established in the initial account. Responsibility, duress and hospital-versus-prison outcomes are simplified philosophical examples, not jurisdiction-specific legal guidance.
- Gage's personality change is the familiar simplified source account. Whitman's tumor is reported with the source's explicit uncertainty about causation. Addiction, OCD and brain-injury examples illustrate constraints without establishing any person's diagnosis or automatic legal incapacity.
- Zhuangzi is described as approximately contemporary with Epictetus although they lived centuries apart. Spinoza is early modern, not ancient. The source's claim that determinism has the shortest history and fewest adherents is unsupported and sits uneasily with its own Stoic account. Quotations are new translations of the supplied Chinese, not verified standard English editions.
- The summaries of quantum randomness, libertarianism, hard determinism and compatibilism are introductory and contested. Determinism does not require an experimenter to predict choices with 100 percent accuracy; hard determinists may still support preventive sanctions, and libertarians need not deny mitigation. The religious examples are attributed beliefs, not empirical causal claims.
- The Libet timing appears to conflate readiness-potential onset before movement with the interval before reported conscious intention. Haynes prediction accuracy is explicitly qualified in the source. Schurger's accumulation model and Libet's proposed veto are interpretations, not settled demonstrations of free will or its absence. The arbitrary-task versus meaningful-choice limitation is retained. Statements covering all conscious decisions overgeneralize the described experiments.
- App reward mechanisms and the final perfect-prediction machine are illustrative claims/scenarios. Freedom, dignity and responsibility conclusions remain philosophical positions; they are not established by these examples alone.

## 062 — What makes someone still the same person?

- Grandma Chen's family, the teletransporter and divided-brain procedure are teaching scenarios. The source explicitly suspends medical feasibility for brain division; neither brain copying nor continuity of consciousness is established technology. The early cell-turnover account is a broad simplification: not every tissue or cell type is replaced on the stated schedule.
- Alzheimer's descriptions are illustrative, not diagnostic criteria. Loss of decision-making capacity, personal identity and human dignity are separate issues; the opposing relatives' arguments must not be read as endorsing dehumanization or as clinical care instructions. The closing questions about responding to mistaken memories are philosophical exercises, not individualized dementia-care guidance.
- The source presents advance directives as the common default and says the law must choose between past and present selves. Actual legal scope, applicability, capacity assessment and surrogate decision-making differ by jurisdiction and circumstances. Its generalization that institutions mostly follow psychological continuity is in tension with the later bodily-continuity account of criminal responsibility. Both are preserved as source claims, not legal advice.
- The Zhuangzi passage is newly translated from the supplied Chinese. Calling it a firsthand record does not establish a historical dream report. Its interpretation through full memories and feelings adds modern epistemological language. The Chinese Nagasena text and Pali Milindapanha have related but non-identical textual histories; the dialogue is paraphrased, not a verified transcript.
- Bodily, psychological and relational identity are introductory philosophical positions. The source's claim that a teletransported person's experiences simply continue presupposes the identity question later contested. Identity's one-to-one constraint is a philosophical/logical premise, not a medical result. Parfit's account and Buddhist not-self have affinities without being identical doctrines; the five-aggregate river analogy is pedagogical.
- In the Parfit ethics comparison, the Chinese switches between 'your future self' and 'my present self'; this pronoun shift is retained. In the four-level analysis, its description of the upcoming comparison as the 'second level' does not align neatly with the levels just enumerated. Claims that digital traces outlast bodies, memorial accounts implement relational identity, or AI relationships are strictly one-way are interpretive generalizations, not guarantees about services or consciousness.

## 063 — What makes an action right?

- The Mignonette account mixes a historical legal case with reconstructed sensory detail, dialogue and biographical claims. The timeline of drinking seawater, killing and rescue, the German rescuer's ship type, bringing the knife back as evidence, Parker's family's unforgiving reaction, and the source's assertion that an admitting defendant cannot believe himself guilty all require review against records. The sentences were commuted; the source calls the outcome a pardon. Its certainty that Parker would die that day and everyone else must die without the killing exceeds what such a situation establishes. The earlier calculation changes from three lives versus none to three versus one. These source claims are preserved.
- Health/safety: the seawater-drinking account warns of harm, but 'slow suicide' is the source's rhetorical characterization, not a clinical description or a claim of Parker's suicidal intent. Soaking injured skin in seawater is narrated historical conduct, not endorsed first-aid guidance. Killing a person for food or organs is an ethical counterexample, not an instruction. Triage prioritization is simplified and does not make killing an uninvolved person equivalent to allocating scarce treatment; clinical criteria and legal rules vary.
- In the virtue-ethics phone example, the source says the finder does **not** return the phone, then uses 'I am not that kind of person' to illustrate virtuous character. This apparent negation error is deliberately preserved. The source also says the three explanations cannot all be right, then says each holds part of the truth; these levels may be compatible but the transition is not explained.
- Mohism, Confucian role ethics, utilitarianism, deontology, virtue ethics and care ethics are teaching summaries, not exhaustive doctrines. Imagined judgments by Mozi or Confucians are conjectures. Bentham/Mill's maxim, equal consideration versus equal shares, Kant's universalization test, the means/end distinction, graded care and Gilligan's gender findings are more nuanced than their compressed presentation. Consequentialism need not require selfish motives or explicit calculation, so the Mencius passage does not by itself refute utilitarianism.
- The Mencius and Analects quotations are new translations of the supplied Chinese. The Mencius phrase interpreted here as disliking the child's cries has also been read as concern about a bad reputation; the source's chosen interpretation is retained. 'Before any ism was named' and the eighteen-hundred-year interval are rhetorical/approximate chronology.
- Foot's original trolley case and later switch variants should be distinguished. In the bridge case, the source equates needing a person's body to stop the trolley with needing that person's death; death need not itself be the means if stopping could occur with survival. The source gives only part of the doctrine of double effect, not its full conditions. Trolley response regularity, two-system moral psychology and the organ case's implications for all consequentialists are disputed generalizations, not universal results.
- Omission liability differs across legal systems, duties and circumstances; the source's generalization is not legal guidance. Autonomous-driving examples and Moral Machine preferences do not establish that deployed vehicles explicitly rank victim categories or turn trolley dilemmas into ordinary engineering practice. The source's next paragraph appropriately distinguishes real system failures from these imagined forced choices. Allocation committees and public rules are illustrative institutional responses, not a description of every existing system.
