# Source review notes — Liberal Arts chapters 51–65

These are issues noticed during translation, not a comprehensive historical,
religious, legal, or scientific fact-check. Source claims are retained unless
an explicitly labeled translator note says otherwise.

## Chapter 51 — Islam

- The chapter distinguishes scripture, remembered tradition, historical evidence,
  and the author's evaluation. The translation preserves those distinctions;
  religious assertions are not presented as the translator's beliefs.
- The coin reform is described as abolishing all older image-bearing money,
  Arabian society as lacking states, and early conquests as already spanning
  three continents within a generation. These sweeping statements need nuance.
- The Karbala account says everyone in Husayn's party was killed, overlooking
  survivors. Succession narratives and the development of Sunni/Shia/Kharijite
  identities are substantially compressed.
- Ramadan fasting is described as sunrise-to-sunset rather than dawn-to-sunset.
  The identical-white-clothing claim overlooks differing pilgrimage dress, and
  zakat's conditions and exemptions are simplified. Retained source descriptions
  are not instructions for religious observance.
- The House of Wisdom/translation movement, Rumi's founding role, the Mihna's
  political consequences, and sharia/fiqh distinctions are simplified. The
  source says ships travel “westwards” to Guangzhou/Quanzhou; the directional
  inconsistency is retained rather than silently corrected.
- Present-day population proportions, pilgrimage attendance, Indonesia's distance,
  and extremism-related generalisations are source-edition claims, not current
  measurements independently verified by this translation.
- Quran passages are translated from the Chinese text attributed to Ma Jian,
  not substituted with a separately published English Quran translation.

## Chapter 52 — India's religious traditions

- The shop and riverbank scenes are illustrative composites, not documented
  interviews. The source calls a rite a sunrise ceremony and then says it was
  performed the previous night and will recur the next night; retained.
- Accounts of the name Hinduism, colonial classification, doctrinal continuity,
  Sanskritization, and the relations among Indian philosophical schools are
  broad interpretive summaries. Their competing explanations remain distinct.
- Scriptural passages are fresh translations of the supplied Chinese
  paraphrases, not quotations imported from published English translations.
  The presentation of Rig Veda 10.129 as ending at the quoted point needs source
  review; the chapter's reading of 1.164 is an interpretation, not a neutral
  resolution of its possible meanings.
- The attribution of the birth-versus-conduct verse to Dhammapada 396 appears
  questionable and needs verification. The classification of Manusmriti with
  dharma-sutra texts also needs specialist review. Neither attribution has been
  silently replaced in the translation.
- Ambedkar's declaration is explicitly paraphrased in the source. Its caste
  terminology describes discriminatory classifications, not translator-endorsed
  labels for people.
- Statements about constitutional protections, reservations, present-day
  politics, and population are source-edition claims, not independently updated
  legal guidance or current measurements. The source's own phrase “verified
  fact” is retained and does not assert an additional translator fact-check.
- The theology/religious-studies contrast, depictions of particular believers,
  and the application of Smart's dimensions to people who identify as
  nonreligious are the author's simplifying framework, not universal definitions.

## Chapter 53 — Buddhism across Asia

- The translation workshop and reported disputes are narrated reconstructions.
  Xuanzang's travel, translation teams, and the Guanshiyin/Guanzizai naming issue
  are compressed; the source's simple “earlier mistranslation” account should
  not replace philological review of variant Sanskrit names and manuscripts.
- Chinese vocabulary examples are given in romanization with English glosses
  so the argument about Chinese word histories remains intelligible. Claims
  that all listed terms originated in Buddhist translation, and the simplified
  account of geyi, need etymological review.
- Verses from Han Yu, the Heart Sutra, and the Platform Sutra are translated
  directly from the supplied Chinese. The source distinguishes familiar
  Platform Sutra wording from earlier Dunhuang manuscript readings; retained.
- The monastic community, lay precepts, Chan/Pure Land/Vajrayana contrasts,
  Huineng succession story, Bodhidharma chronology, and Maitreya/Budai imagery
  are broad or traditional accounts, not uniform descriptions of every school.
- Ashoka's dharma is presented through the source's Buddhist framing. Temple
  tax exemptions, persecution totals, Japanese introduction narratives, and
  “one decree” accounts of Meiji separation compress complex institutions and
  events. The source's three explanations of Buddhist change remain separate.
- The disappearance of Buddhism from India, Nalanda's destruction, absence of
  village roots, and claims of universal ordination or uniquely Tibetan
  reincarnation institutions are sweeping historical generalizations.
- The source calls Jon Kabat-Zinn a doctor/physician; his professional title
  requires biographical verification. Its depiction of mindfulness programs
  as attention training alone and its clinical-benefit claims are retained
  source characterizations, not treatment advice or an independent efficacy
  review. Present-day app, robot, AI, and participation claims are not updated.

## Chapter 54 — East Asian ritual and popular belief

- The hillside family, teacher, and quoted responses are illustrative scenes.
  April 5 is the scene's date, not a rule that Qingming always falls on that date.
- The chapter's categorical statements about death, consciousness, divine
  existence, and ritual efficacy belong to the source's argument. Descriptions
  of ritual healing are not medical recommendations or substitutes for care.
- Confucian, Buddhist, Daoist, Christian, Muslim, and regional practices are
  broadly contrasted. Their assigned “service” roles and the claim that belief
  is optional and belonging absent in East Asia are analytical simplifications,
  not descriptions of every practitioner or institution.
- The claim that Japanese has no word corresponding to conversion needs
  correction or substantiation in the Chinese edition; retained, not endorsed.
  Spirit-money geography, Roman inclusion of deities, festival origins, and
  imperial monopolies/exemptions also require historical and regional nuance.
- Analects excerpts are freshly translated from the supplied Chinese. The
  reading of “as if present” as locating the true recipient in the participant
  is the author's interpretation, not the only possible reading.
- C. K. Yang's institutional/diffused distinction is a theoretical framework;
  the near-universal participation claims are not a new demographic estimate.
- Claimed app-usage surges, sharing totals, temple trends, psychological effects,
  and contemporary church debates are source-edition claims, not independently
  measured or updated here. The source's steelmanned arguments remain labeled
  as arguments rather than translator conclusions.

## Chapter 55 — Traditions without written scriptures

- The first section says “drum” in its heading but describes a mask; both are
  retained. The museum object and qasgiq scene are illustrative, not a supplied
  accession record or documented performance. They should not be used to infer
  actual provenance, consent, or access rights for a particular sacred object.
- The chapter's descriptions of Yup'ik, Inuit, Kwakwaka'wakw, Yoruba, Maori,
  Quechua, Ainu, Arrernte, Anangu, and other peoples concern distinct traditions;
  the translation does not merge them into a single generic Indigenous religion.
  Broad assertions about oral transmission, gender restrictions, obligatory
  memorization of the full Ifa corpus, and historical outsider-record dominance
  require community-specific nuance.
- The purported Seattle speech excerpt is translated from the user-supplied
  Chinese and explicitly followed by the source's disputed-attribution analysis.
  The dates and reconstruction of the Seattle/Smith/Perry transmission chain
  require verification; the excerpt is not presented as authenticated speech.
- The source's account of Dreamtime as simply a mistranslation, Hunt's linguistic
  biography, Tylor's evolutionary ladder, Descola's four types, and Eliade/Harner
  generalizations are simplified scholarly interpretations, not comprehensive
  accounts of those debates or theories.
- Wounded Knee's event sequence, death toll, Ghost Dance participation, and
  causal connection to official misreading are compressed and require historical
  review. The narrative should not be treated as a complete incident account.
- The 1990 U.S. repatriation law, Mabo, the Uluru climbing closure, and Standing
  Rock are source-edition summaries, not current legal advice. Scope, eligibility,
  decision-making authorities, and the claim that law sided with sacred-site
  recognition “for the first time” need qualification. No contemporary legal
  update or museum repatriation determination has been performed here.

## Chapter 56 — Religion, doubt, and secularization

- The amulet and Western Residence scenes are narrative reconstructions.
  Fan Zhen passages and Analects quotations are translated from the supplied
  Chinese, not replaced by a published English translation. The source's
  chronology and motives assigned to historical participants need source review.
  In particular its conditional about Fan Zhen failing to answer well and
  dismantling the explanatory framework is awkward; the English retains that
  conditional rather than reversing it into a successful challenge.
- The source defines atheism narrowly as asserting nonexistence, deism as a
  wholly nonintervening creator, and places deism under “disbelief.” These are
  its teaching categories, not exhaustive definitions of those positions.
- Fan Zhen's knife/sharpness analogy is presented as his philosophical argument,
  not an independently established neuroscientific proof. Claims about ancient
  Charvaka chronology and textual survival also need historical qualification.
- Science/religion examples are deliberately compared through two explanatory
  models. Clerical status, institutional origins of universities, Galileo trial
  motives, and the Copernicus/Lemaitre narratives are compressed source accounts.
- France's 1905 and 2004 laws, Indian secularism, the U.S. Constitution, UDHR
  Article 18, and Chinese constitutional Article 36 are translated source-edition
  summaries, not independently updated legal advice. In particular the 1905
  law's relation to all religious signs in public institutions is overbroad.
  Stated legal protections do not themselves establish uniform enforcement.
- Weber, Berger, and Taylor are paraphrased by the source. Their arguments are
  not direct quotations from published English works; claimed trends in church
  attendance, religiosity, Nordic trust, and spiritual consumption are not new
  measurements. Universal claims about premodern belief and modern choice need
  geographical and historical qualification.
