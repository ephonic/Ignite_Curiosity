# Source inconsistencies retained — Chapters 057–059

These notes identify issues noticed while translating the supplied Chinese fragments. They are not a comprehensive independent revision or fact-check. Source arguments, qualifications and examples remain in the English; historical and conceptual caveats are recorded here rather than silently rewriting the text.

## Chapter 057 — Doing philosophy

- The answer-sheet opening's size, scanning speed and assertion that every worthwhile question is already asked are illustrative, not specifications for an actual examination system.
- The399BCEtrial scene compresses proceedings, omitting Socrates's later proposed fine after the civic-maintenance suggestion. 'No examiner' is an analogy, not absence of prosecutors. Corrupting youth is declared the essential charge interpretively, and the prospect of acquittal through promising silence is counterfactual, not an established offered bargain.
- Socrates is said never to have written a word, although no philosophical writings survive and Plato's Phaedo attributes prison verse-making to him. His output of answers as literally zero and the familiar 'I know only that I know nothing' formula are rhetorical simplifications of the source's pedagogical contrast.
- Philosophers' positions are reduced to slogans; Zhuangzi's equality of things and Kant's persons-as-ends are not full formulations. Memorisation first as a uniform millennia-long Chinese path and public disputation as the sole basis of medieval degrees oversimplify varied institutions. Chan blows/shouts are described pedagogically, not endorsed as educational practice.
- Gongsun Long's white-horse argument is given one simplified scope interpretation. The claim seven in ten disagreements disappear on clarification is rhetorical, not a cited statistic.
- The supposedly true premise **all cats have tails** has counterexamples including tailless cats. The example still illustrates invalid inference, but its announced two true premises are not universally true. Indian claim/reason/example structure is a simplified presentation, not the complete range of Indian logical forms.
- Success's revised probability claim does not follow merely from one failed hard worker; similarly one successful online learner does not refute every population-level internet-effect claim. The source presents these as universal assertions to illustrate counterexamples.
- The fish dialogue's an zhi ambiguity is preserved with pinyin plus the two English glosses. Huizi's logical victory and Zhuangzi's equivocation are the source's reading, followed by its own alternative sympathetic reading, not uncontested scholarship or a measurable two-thousand-year majority verdict.
- The settled-disputes paragraph treats bloodletting as categorically discredited, overlooking limited modern therapeutic phlebotomy indications, and treats falling speed without specifying vacuum/drag. These are historical illustrations, not medical advice.
- Logical positivism and Wittgensteinian therapy are grouped without distinguishing their substantial differences. Logic's departure from philosophy and all experimental frameworks presupposing personal identity are broad disciplinary claims. Rival explanations need not be as mutually exclusive as the source's comparison declares.
- Cartesian doubt combines examples and works; the demon is called omnipotent, and the leap from thinking to a substantive self remains philosophically contestable. Brain-in-vat imaginability is said to prove possibility, itself a disputed modal inference.
- Nearly all conspiracy theorists are said to begin from total doubt, an unsupported generalisation. The school penalty, online conformity and answer-market passages likewise state broad tendencies categorically.
- Chatbots are described as knowing every philosophical answer, equally fluent regardless of accuracy and unable to mass-produce good questions. These are the source's rhetorical/temporally situated capability claims, retained rather than independently updated or treated as guarantees.

## Chapter 058 — Knowledge

- The stopped-watch case begins with a long-stopped watch at the back of a drawer but later treats it as ordinarily trusted; justified ignorance of its failure is assumed rather than specified. Its exact-to-the-second match twice daily depends on display precision. The familiar clock case predates Gettier; the source calls it a typical Gettier-style case, not one of his original examples.
- The Meno scene is a literary reconstruction with added courtyard, sweat and onlookers. Socrates's geometrical questioning is called never supplying an answer, though the dialogue's leading constructions and assent questions complicate that claim. The Larissa example is simplified to blind guessing versus personal travel, rather than every possible basis of true opinion.
- Justified true belief is repeatedly presented as an unchanged two-thousand-year mainstream definition. That conventional historical story overstates agreement and continuity; Plato's dialogues do not simply endorse the modern tripartite account. Its conditions' necessity is also treated as settled despite philosophical objections to each in some theories.
- Over90per-cent reliance on testimony and90per-cent of everyday knowledge being only reasonable belief are unreferenced pedagogical figures. The eyewitness-only position is later renamed believing everything's opponent, although reliance on others does not imply indiscriminate belief.
- The inference example oddly contrasts not seeing the **Sun's far side** with concluding **Earth rotates**. This unsupported connection is preserved. The five wells overlap, with expert testimony explicitly a subtype rather than an independent exhaustive category.
- Nyāya's pramāṇa comparison is simplified, and sensory confidence/error is generalised without conditions of initial confidence, interviewing, delay or corroboration. A visually represented border need not reveal its legal status; the impossibility of knowing one's country by perception alone is rhetorical.
- Knowing is first said to demand a full-strength warrant and later contrasted with science's probabilistic practice. Fallibilism allows knowledge without certainty, so the final claim that almost nobody has everyday knowledge is a contested standard, not forced by Gettier cases. Confidence levels, event probabilities and clinical efficacy percentages are also distinct quantities, although grouped as scales.
- Confucius's final zhi can be read as wisdom/knowing; the source deliberately interprets it as second-order knowledge. Its alignment with Socratic ignorance is an interpretive comparison. The fish-dialogue excerpt stops before Huizi's rejoinder and Zhuangzi's wordplay, producing a different argumentative emphasis from Chapter057's full exchange.
- No-false-premise repair is criticised chiefly for practical unverifiability, leaving logical counterexamples unstated. Reliabilism's barn example depends on how reliability is individuated, as the source itself acknowledges. The no-recipe view mixes different forms of knowing without resolving whether its target is propositional knowledge alone.
- Water's100°Cboiling point lacks a pressure condition. Triangle angles summing to180degrees is declared absolute/universal without limiting it to Euclidean geometry. Both source mathematical/scientific qualifications remain omitted rather than silently repaired.
- Al-Ghazālī, Zhuangzi and Descartes are compared through radical doubt while their aims and recoveries differ. Cartesian arguments are pedagogical paraphrases; Kant's synthesis does not straightforwardly guarantee a particular future sunrise, and time/space versus causation are not the same kind of Kantian structure.
- The AI paragraph definitively denies models belief and calls this a Gettier sequel, although whether computational systems have belief is contested and a missing-belief case is not a standard Gettier case satisfying all three conditions. These remain the source's philosophical claims, not independent claims about this assistant's experience.
- Visual deception is described as systematic for the **first time** with generated imagery despite older manipulation and staged evidence. Conspiracy chains are said to appear truth-qualified despite hidden false premises; apparent satisfaction differs from actual truth. The source's recommendations concern epistemic checking, not medical or legal decisions.

## Chapter 059 — Perception

- The blind-spot exercise lacks a sufficiently fixed target geometry and full instruction to occlude the other eye; its success is not guaranteed as described. Peripheral colour discrimination is reduced to near absence, and the text's universal successful grasping/no collisions claims are rhetorical overstatements.
- Film motion, the Müller–Lyer illusion and the moon illusion are simplified perceptual examples; same-night photographs require comparable focal length and framing, with small real angular-size changes ignored. The chequer-shadow illustration is described but not supplied as a new figure in either source or translation.
- The classroom's connecting strip is described as uniformly changing without seams despite intended constant colour. A/B digital grey values or luminance are sometimes conflated with perceived surface colour; a projected image is described in terms of physically reflected light without equipment qualifications.
- Dreams are repeatedly said to contain **no external input** and to be universally unrecognised as dreams, explicitly saying recognised dreams would not be dreams. External stimulus incorporation and lucid dreaming contradict these categorical claims. The translation retains them rather than inserting scientific corrections.
- The Yan Hui rice story is introduced as a recorded traditional account, not independently authenticated eyewitness history. Japanese ao/ao shingō supplies romanisation and English colour glosses for the source's essential word-boundary example; no Han glyphs are required.
- Direct realism is equated with an unmediated broadcast and representationalism with looking at an internal picture, simplified forms that do not exhaust these positions. Predictive processing's hierarchical signalling account is treated as an explanatory model and then appropriately questioned, not a verified exclusive mechanism for every listed phenomenon.
- The Zhuangzi dream quotation is translated separately from its explanatory paraphrase; the paraphrase adds lying stiffly in bed, which is not straightforwardly the quoted wording. Its final transformation-of-things interpretation is retained. The text groups Plato's cave, Cartesian doubt, Buddhist impermanence and Putnam's semantic project while explicitly distinguishing some purposes.
- The faulty-instrument explanation's assertion that merely learning an illusion's cause calibrates a device is not generally valid: information is not physical repair. The 'always overcorrection' direction claim overgeneralises across illusions. Evolutionary interface theory is unnamed in the source and presented as a contested interpretation, not consensus.
- Pyrrho's dangerous-road anecdotes retain the explicit late-source/likely-caricature caveat. The assertion that an unliveable philosophy must be incomplete or insincere is the source's normative challenge, not a refutation of all ancient sceptical practice.
- The microscopy paragraph combines modern attainable scale with Leeuwenhoek's early instruments and a broad first-microorganisms priority claim. Cochlear implants are said to bypass the ear outright, simplifying their specific bypass of damaged sensory hair-cell function and stimulation of the auditory nerve.
- Deepfake video is said unable to serve alone as evidence, and personalised feeds are analogised to brain predictions; evidentiary weight depends on provenance/context and the analogy does not establish identical mechanisms. The final bodyguard/deceiver split of half the time is figurative, inconsistent with the earlier assertion perception is usually accurate if read as a statistic.
