# Source inconsistencies retained: chapters 020–050

These notes identify issues encountered while translating the Chinese source; they are not a comprehensive historical, literary, or scientific revision. The translation retains the source's arguments and examples. Chapter references use the original fragment numbers, independent of numbering in partial preview PDFs.

## Chapter 020 — Poetry

- “Three traditions” introduces four stops. The heading and all four discussions are retained.
- The compression test for Wang Wei's “Birdsong Brook” discusses the sound *jian* (“between”), which does not occur in the quoted poem. Its account of five-character lines also describes four-step rhythmic units. These observations are retained as written.
- The same analysis says the poem supplies no subject and never explicitly says “quiet,” although its opening includes *ren* (“person”) and its second line includes *jing* (“still”). The source's categorical wording is retained.
- “Five frequent companions” introduces only four named devices: imagery, metaphor, symbolism, and repetition.
- The “Guan Ju” discussion calls its Chinese prose compression seven characters, although the quoted Chinese sentence has twelve. It twice calls the quoted stanza sixteen characters, although the four quoted eight-character lines total thirty-two. Counts remain those of the source, explicitly identified as Chinese-character counts in English.

## Chapter 021 — Drama

- The ancient theatre opening is a reconstructed narrative, not a cited eyewitness account. Its seating, attendance, audience composition, and prisoner-release details remain source claims.
- The account of mythic endings being fixed is categorical; the translation does not introduce qualifications about variations between dramatic treatments.

## Chapter 022 — The novel

- The source simplifies the forms of *Pamela* as the maid's letters throughout and *The Sound and the Fury* as alternating narration by brothers. These descriptions do not discuss the former's journal/editorial material or the latter's final third-person section.
- Assertions contrasting the novel's access to ordinary private lives with earlier genres retain the source's broad scope rather than introducing an independent literary-history revision.

## Chapter 023 — Rewriting old stories

- The intertextuality section calls Xin Qiji's quoted allusion seven characters; the supplied Chinese quotation has eight. The translation retains the source's count.
- The account of *Journey to the West* says “dozens of centuries,” despite its own chronology running from the Tang pilgrimage to a Ming novel. This duration is retained.
- The hero's-journey outline combines stages associated with later practical adaptations of Campbell's theory while attributing the outline directly to Campbell. The translation preserves the source's presentation.
- The final recap calls the four Wu River readings four poems, although the first is Sima Qian's prose account. The original inconsistency is retained.
- The cultural-appropriation paragraph groups “Africa's Lion King stories” with traditional narratives without distinguishing the film's modern story from its African setting. This wording is retained, not presented as independently researched provenance.

## Chapter 024 — Canon formation

- The chapter describes Murasaki Shikibu's environment as excluding women from Chinese-language education categorically; it does not distinguish institutional conventions from individual women's knowledge of Chinese.
- Its explanations of Zhong Rong's ranking and Su Shi's praise simplify their historical and critical contexts. In particular, the account connects Su Shi's extensive poems matching Tao's rhymes with the Huangzhou demotion without distinguishing later stages of his life.
- The online-literature section says there is no editorial review, then describes contracts and publication checkpoints. These source statements are retained together.
- The source calls Mo Yan's 2012 award the first Nobel recognition of Chinese-language writing in a novelist's capacity, overlooking Gao Xingjian's 2000 award and novels. The translation retains this claim rather than silently substituting a different nationality-based distinction.

## Chapter 025 — Interpretation and evidence

- The source gives 1968 as the publication year of Barthes's “The Death of the Author,” without distinguishing its earlier English publication from its French appearance. The translation retains the date.
- The “Quiet Night Thoughts” discussion treats its selected early textual tradition and subsequent editorial alteration as a categorical account of transmission; its own question about access to Li Bai's original remains open. The variants and their distinct interpretations are preserved.
- The discussion describes AI-generated summaries as the average of previous interpretations. This is retained as the source's explanatory metaphor, not an independently established technical account of model operation.

## Chapter 026 — Human origins and migration

- The reconstructed French scene forty thousand years ago includes manufacture of an Acheulean-style handaxe as a grave offering alongside other behavioural details. This is retained as the source's imagined scene, not archaeological documentation of that particular combination.
- The source treats planned manufacture, grammar and jokes, grave offerings as evidence of an afterlife concept, and some specific care practices with greater certainty than their described material evidence alone establishes. Its later distinction between evidence types is also preserved.
- Human evolutionary relationships are simplified into separate branches; the text's categorical treatment of erectus versus sapiens ancestry is not a comprehensive account of hypotheses about African ancestral populations.
- Descriptions of archaic ancestry as belonging to everyone outside Africa simplify variation among individuals and populations and do not discuss gene flow back into Africa. Approximate dates and proportions remain those of the source, not updated research estimates.

## Chapter 027 — Cave paintings

- The opening treats ochre as useless beyond colour and explicitly rules out medicinal uses; later reasoning treats costly pigment use as outside practical utility. The source does not examine potential practical uses of ochre.
- The account of disinterested aesthetics moves between lack of immediate subsistence utility, ritual motivation, and Kant's aesthetic criterion. These are the source's argumentative connections rather than equivalent concepts established by the translation.
- The Makapansgat pebble's carrier and motivation are stated confidently before being discussed as inference. The source's distinction between physical evidence and inferred intention remains applicable to its own account.
- Rock-art dates are retained as approximate source figures; the chapter is not updated to incorporate later discoveries or revised dating. Its table retains all sites, remains, dates, and the original `llX` column specification.

## Chapter 028 — Agricultural origins

- The opening attributes the diagnostic rice detachment scar to a charred grain without distinguishing the spikelet base used in domestication studies. It presents a single hypothetical specimen as firmly domesticated within a broadly dated regional process.
- The Abu Hureyra reconstruction combines an approximate eleven-thousand-year date with a categorical pre-agricultural setting. Its buildings, tasks, inherited sickle, and family scenes remain reconstructed narrative, not evidence for one documented morning.
- Hunter-gatherer working-hour estimates are reported specifically as food acquisition, but the surrounding leisure comparison does not separately discuss processing, childcare, or other work. Source generalisations about early farmers' health and domestication chronology are retained, not made universal by independent research.
- The final proposed lesson that hesitant non-adopters left no descendants conflicts with the chapter's own surviving non-agricultural examples. It remains one of the source's rhetorical alternatives, not an endorsed conclusion.

## Chapter 029 — Cities

- The Uruk street scene combines an approximate 5,200-year date with walls, named ration entries, and other institutional details as a reconstructed composite, not a directly evidenced single street scene.
- The division-of-labour argument categorically says villagers all farm and urban craftspeople cannot perform one another's occupations. These are the source's simplified contrasts.
- The account of Mohenjo-daro describes nearly every house as possessing both bathing facilities and a well. Its subsequent suggestions about governing institutions are explicitly hypotheses; absent identifiable royal monuments do not themselves prove absence of kings.
- The John Snow account implies removal of the Broad Street pump handle caused the outbreak's decline and calls it a city's first numerical identification of disease origin. It does not distinguish an already declining outbreak or earlier public-health investigations.

## Chapter 030 — Writing

- The 3200 BCE Uruk reconstruction describes fully wedge-shaped cuneiform production. It does not distinguish early proto-cuneiform practices from subsequent wedge-dominated script.
- The overview says writing left warehouses within several centuries, then gives Hammurabi's much later code among the illustrations. Its specific Sumerian school story is also described as five thousand years old without dating the surviving text separately.
- The complaint-tablet discussion attributes survival specifically to firing, without supplying evidence of that treatment for this particular object. Its survival explanation is retained as the source's account.
- The concluding maxim that poems outlive lists sits uneasily beside the chapter's evidence for exceptionally durable administrative records. Both are retained.
- Categorical descriptions of quipu as non-writing and the extent to which literacy enables otherwise impossible thinking are retained alongside the source's own qualifications and counterexamples.

## Chapter 031 — States

- The opening uses rough costs, buying power, and banknote acceptance as an illustrative contemporary scenario, not verified pricing or legal advice.
- The claim that the earliest components of the state were ledgers rather than weapons, and regional state-origin dates, are broad source interpretations rather than independently established universal sequences.
- The law-code section first presents public written rules as practically checkable by everyone, then explains limited evidence for using Hammurabi's provisions as a court manual. Both perspectives remain in the translation.
- The final claim that nearly all descendants of Zomian highlanders have moved down from the mountains is sweeping and unsourced. It is retained as part of the source's closing question.

## Chapter 032 — Ritual and invisible order

- The source gives “three thousand years later” between Upper Cave burials tens of thousands of years ago and Shang burials, rather than a duration compatible with its own dates.
- Its Aztec paragraph places Spanish conquerors “centuries later” after a sixteenth-century setting. This chronology is retained.
- The Analects discussion says “in the same chapter” while moving from “Eight Rows” to “Yong Ye.” Both explicit references and the inconsistent connective are retained.
- The athletes paragraph contains contradictory wording about knowing ritual effects. The English renders its evident surrounding argument: awareness that ritual does not determine outcomes can coexist with reassurance from controllable actions.
- The character used to explain the shape of divination cracks is retained twice as a protected glyph with pronunciation and English explanation; it is an essential source example, not untranslated prose.

## Chapter 033 — Trade

- The Old Babylonian Ur reconstruction includes burning camel dung without distinguishing evidence for camel use at that date. The sensory scene remains the source's reconstruction.
- The account of Phoenician letters treats merchant accounting as their origin and describes every English letter as an ancestral merchant's shorthand. The translation preserves this simplified history, including its omission of intermediate script developments.
- The Ye Xian summary simplifies who discovers the shoe and the ruler who marries her; no independent retelling has replaced the supplied account.
- The final bronze-making scene refers to participation in an event spanning two or three centuries, without explaining that duration. It remains unchanged.
- Modern component origins and trade figures are retained as source examples, not verified descriptions of every phone or updated supply-chain statistics.

## Chapter 034 — Early empires

- The law discussion calls eighteenth-century BCE Hammurabi earlier than all empires covered, although the same chapter dates Akkad to around 2300 BCE.
- The comparison of Qin's unification record with Cyrus's cylinder calls them roughly contemporary despite a gap of over three centuries. The text also describes the Akkad-to-Qin span as over one thousand years rather than over two thousand.
- The explanation of Qin's measures calls *shi* a capacity measure in this passage, without distinguishing its weight sense in the quoted formula. Its simplified axle-gauge and script account remains as supplied.
- The source's European Union example gives nineteen euro-using countries, an outdated count retained rather than silently updated.
- Historical administrative practices, and Assyrian versus Qin infrastructural reach, are broad comparative interpretations. The five-column `llllX` table retains every row, date, practice, and stance; layout adjustment is handled separately without changing content.

## Chapter 035 — The Axial Age

- The Chen–Cai reconstruction's 489 BCE setting, description of Confucius as nearly sixty, and duration of his travels do not fit neatly together. These source details are retained rather than harmonised.
- The passage about Athens dates the vote to execute Socrates to the third century BCE. The source's century is retained, although his execution is conventionally dated to 399 BCE.
- The causal-reasoning exercise moves from shared features among selected cases to calling them necessary conditions more confidently than those comparisons establish. Its prompts and conclusions remain intact.
- Broad claims that the Axial Age first introduced criticism of power or interior moral life coexist with the source's later qualifications about earlier traditions. Both are preserved.

## Chapter 036 — Ritual, benevolence, and coercion

- The dance hierarchy assumes a fixed row width when stating the numbers of performers at each rank. No alternative historical interpretation is substituted.
- The discussions of filial remonstrance, agreement between Mencius and Xunzi, and the near-zero cost of internalised norms preserve the source's strong interpretive claims, together with its later discussion of coercive social costs.
- The source calls Qin the state Han Fei served. This conflates the Han prince and envoy's relationship to Han with his arrival and imprisonment in Qin; its wording is retained.
- The Lu Xun discussion treats cannibalism categorically as metaphor, without distinguishing the story's literal narrative motif from its social interpretation.
- The source-essential character-shape examples for *xi* (practise) and *yu* (feathers) are retained as protected glyphs with English explanation, not untranslated prose.

## Chapter 037 — Rules and Daoism

- The account places Diogenes in a wooden barrel rather than distinguishing the large ceramic storage vessel associated with the tradition. The supplied material is retained.
- After quoting four passages from the Daodejing, the source refers to “these three passages.” The inconsistent count is retained.
- The claim that almost every destruction of old conventions is followed by more brutal power, and the statement that hundreds of millions practise digital fasting, are sweeping source claims rather than independently verified historical or current statistics.
- The readings of non-action, language, and recommendation algorithms are the source's philosophical comparisons, not equivalent technical definitions. In particular, the categorical claim that an algorithm cannot register absence is retained as part of its cup analogy.

## Chapter 038 — The Hundred Schools

- The opening dates Mo Di's journey to the late fifth century BCE, while the reconstructed scene specifies about 440 BCE. Both source dates are retained.
- The Fu Tun story identifies the pardoning Qin ruler as king without distinguishing the ruler's historically used title in the source tradition.
- The argument about the white horse presents one modern interpretation through classification versus identity, not a settled account of every historical reading. Its “first” easily misjudged counterexample occurs after earlier examples already labelled that way.
- The accounts of private teaching, the end of contention after unification, Mohism's disappearance, and modern examination systems compress complex developments into strong causal claims. The translation retains the source's own qualifications elsewhere.
- The utilitarian example assumes aggregate pleasure without accounting for wider consequences or distinguishing varieties of utilitarianism. The “across the Atlantic” transition to Bentham is retained despite the chapter's Chinese/Indian starting geography.

## Chapter 039 — Buddhism and suffering

- The opening treats different religions' counting devices as independent inventions despite possible historical transmission, and suggests that a bead cannot be missing while also giving varying bead counts and uses. These are source framing claims.
- The traditional biographical scenes, account of Mahavira as Jainism's founder, and broad claims about rebirth as a shared Indian premise remain alongside the source's historical-evidence caveats.
- The sixteen-character verse is later described as twenty characters including punctuation. The supplied visible punctuation does not clearly support that total; the source's count is retained.
- The Hume comparison gives eighteen hundred years and then two thousand years for the interval from the Buddhist beginnings, without a consistent chronology.
- The clinical-mindfulness section calls Jon Kabat-Zinn a doctor without distinguishing a scientific doctorate from medical qualification. Its statement that torment is halved and its claim of the first instrumental evidence for trainable mind are source generalisations, not verified treatment effects. The source's explicit limitations and risk cautions remain intact.
- The accounts of Mahayana postponing nirvana, Soto as gradual versus Rinzai as sudden, and the modern relaxed attitude as a Charvaka analogue are simplified interpretive contrasts, not comprehensive descriptions of those traditions.

## Chapter 040 — The self

- The opening's near-complete replacement of childhood cells is a simplified biological claim; it does not distinguish long-lived cell populations.
- The source labels *anatta* as both Sanskrit and Pali. This is retained; the usual Sanskrit term differs.
- The hospital narrative is an illustrative reconstruction. Its descriptions of progressive memory loss and severe amnesia should not be read as a universal clinical sequence for every patient.
- The source's reproduction of Nagasena's carriage exchange includes the explanation that a sum of parts is merely a pile. This is retained as the author's paraphrase rather than replaced with another textual translation.
- The body-swap paragraph says copying leads to the “next” machine, but the next numbered experiment is memory loss; copying is third. Both numbering and connective are retained.
- The claim that both duplicates' bodily-continuity ledgers qualify is philosophically contentious given that the Mars body is newly constructed. It remains part of the source's argument.
- Greek, Confucian, and modern-individual blueprints are deliberately broad comparisons; the source's warning that traditions contain internal disagreement is preserved, as are its strong claims about digital mourning and autonomy.

## Chapter 041 — Socratic questioning

- The opening places execution a few days after sentencing, unlike Chapter 042's month. The source also places the counterproposal of a penalty after the death sentence, rather than distinguishing conviction from sentencing.
- Juror eligibility, distribution of identification plaques, and speaking time are simplified. The final modern comparison says Athens gave the defendant a whole day's speech, without separating the day's proceedings from allocated speeches.
- The invented agora exchange presents Spartan exposure of weak infants as a definite legal practice, without marking the evidentiary controversy. It remains part of the source's reconstruction.
- The source's description of the Thirty's victims and population totals, and its inference that softer pleading would probably have saved Socrates, are retained historical interpretations rather than independently established particulars.
- A sentence in the second explanation refers obscurely to “two tyrannical periods.” Its obscurity is retained instead of inventing a missing referent.
- The mathematics counterexample says there is no prime divisible by seven, overlooking seven itself. Its abbreviated prime definition also does not expressly exclude one. Both source statements are retained.
- Claims that Socrates never obtained any definition, that all online proceedings are unchanged except speed, and that any homework answer takes a machine three seconds are rhetorical generalisations in the source.

## Chapter 042 — Plato and Aristotle

- The triangle discussion does not consistently state the Euclidean assumptions behind its angle-sum claim, though its later explanation mentions parallel-line rules. Measurements, ideal objects, and theorem validity are sometimes conflated.
- The apparent daily solar motion is contrasted simply with “the reverse,” without distinguishing Earth's rotation from its revolution.
- In the empirical-truth drawer, the source suggests water in a pressure cooker might boil below 100 degrees. This is retained despite contradicting the usual elevated-pressure example.
- The Confucius mediation paragraph reverses the surrounding Plato/Aristotle characterisations, attributing the corresponding worries about thinking and observing to the opposite sides. Both explicit names and arguments are retained.
- The same Confucius quotation contains fourteen Chinese characters, but the source calls it twelve. Later it places his death only decades before composition of the cave allegory, creating another chronology problem.
- The claim that Plato lived over thirty years after writing the cave, the wood-barrel Diogenes account, and the simplified history of Aristotelian biological classification are retained source details.
- The introductory treatment of force-free bodies, final causes as wishes, and science retaining only efficient causes simplifies physical and philosophical distinctions. The translation does not silently repair it.
- The medieval transmission paragraph foregrounds Arabic scholarship while omitting other routes of preservation and transmission. Its categorical framing remains alongside the chapter's broader anti-exclusivity argument.

## Chapter 043 — Happiness

- The opening retells the second discarded-utensil anecdote as bread eaten without a spoon. This is the supplied retelling, not a replacement from another version of the tradition.
- The scene's heading says Athenian sunshine while its location is Corinth. Alexander is also described there as already conquering much of the known world, without resolving the meeting's usual chronology.
- The Stoic account foregrounds tranquillity rather than clearly separating it from virtue's status, and describes the school as the longest-lived and farthest-travelling among the four without a defined comparison.
- The advertising paragraph first says it switches between Layers Two and Three, then illustrates Layers One and Three. The inconsistent numbering is retained.
- The subtraction explanation states suffering as a difference, then uses numerator and denominator language and reverses the apparent direction of the quantity to reduce. The complete source inconsistency is retained.
- Money thresholds, adaptation times, recovery from disappointment, and the effects of modern psychological methods are broad source claims, not updated research findings or individual treatment predictions.
- The identification of Kahneman's remembering self with the chapter's whole-life virtue layer conflates distinct evaluative constructs. Both the analogy and the chapter's own measurement cautions remain.
- Nozick's experience machine is presented as unending pleasure rather than distinguishing every version of the original thought experiment; this is the source's stipulated scenario.

## Chapter 044 — Justice above law

- The palace narrative categorically calls David's actions legal and compliant with every rule, despite the surrounding account's covenantal prohibitions of adultery and murder. Its administrative-legality framing is retained, not asserted as an independent legal finding.
- The Amos account describes small merchant weights without specifying the transaction or distinguishing undersized measures from inflated price weights. It also calls Judah foreign while identifying the prophet's southern origins; both supplied perspectives remain.
- The translation preserves the source's contrast between ancient Near Eastern kings dispensing justice and Hebrew kings being judged, a broad interpretive contrast rather than a universal claim established across all ancient laws.
- Aquinas is placed two thousand years after Antigone, an overlong interval. Antigone's position is also framed strongly as a kinship exception despite the discussion of universal unwritten obligations.
- Legal positivism is presented as demanding procedural amendment rather than disobedience. This conflates a position about legal validity with positions about moral obligation and permissible resistance; the source's simplified two-camp debate is retained.
- Civil disobedience is said to acquire its name after the described King episode, without recognising earlier usage. Claims about segregation's legality also compress distinctions between different laws and court decisions at that date.
- The legal/just matrix retains its original `llX` columns and all four categories; layout remains subject to separate review.

## Chapter 045 — Classical imperial order

- The coin opening assigns spade-like money to Yan and describes a Zhao trader's knife coins categorically, simplifying regional and chronological coin distributions. Its Qin-to-early-Han duration and examples of nearly contemporary empires also compress substantially different chronologies.
- The Lingqu example moves grain from Lingnan to northern fronts rather than explaining the canal's southern-conquest supply context. This source direction is retained.
- The claim that no small state could build major irrigation or roads is a rhetorical absolute. The depiction of household registration as nearly fixing all status coexists with the source's later merit-rank and manumission examples.
- The higher/lower-status Roman punishment division is presented as newly following the 212 citizenship grant, without distinguishing its earlier development.
- Ashoka and Chen Sheng are described as about a century apart. The thirteenth edict's geographical distribution is also generalised as roadsides across the empire.
- The source lists Kharoshthi alongside Greek and Aramaic as translated forms without distinguishing script from language, and treats Inca writing and wheels categorically.
- The present-day benefits paragraph describes the gaokao as one nationwide paper and curricula as uniform, without regional variation. These source claims are retained rather than updated.
- The source's clear distinction between excavated ordinary-corvee penalties, potential military rules, mobilisation rhetoric, and actual enforcement is preserved; no disputed late-arrival explanation is chosen as fact.

## Chapter 046 — Studying religion

- The Quanzhou street scenes are reconstructed descriptions. Claims about every image prohibition, donation-box absence, and the use of particular surviving mosque spaces are not independently verified architectural or doctrinal inventories.
- The Japanese word for religion is described as newly coined in the nineteenth century, rather than distinguishing earlier lexical uses from its modern translation sense. The same historical paragraph includes modern church weddings without separating periods.
- The seven scales are the chapter's adaptation of Ninian Smart, not his standard seven dimensions: scripture and institutions appear separately while material and narrative dimensions are not independently listed.
- After explicitly rejecting binary classification, the text twice categorically excludes lucky-carp forwarding from religion. Both methodological claim and categorical examples remain.
- The source initially describes Zilu as asking whether spirits exist, although the quoted Analects question asks how to serve them. Its subsequent interpretation treats this as an existence question; the discrepancy is retained.
- The statement that supernatural claims cannot be publicly tested is broader than the later, carefully preserved distinction allowing investigation of observable harms and effects.
- Survey percentages for Japanese religious identification, claims about almost universal funeral practice, and app-download figures lack dates or cited datasets in the source. They remain examples rather than updated findings.
- The Buddhism/salvation and Confucian experience contrasts are simplified; the source's strong cautions against treating any religion as a single voice are preserved alongside them.

## Chapter 047 — Myth

- The rooftop dialogue says Mum made Dad memorise a poem when he was little. That family-role inconsistency is retained, as is the source's relative “last year” for Chang'e 6, anchoring its original scene rather than updating it.
- The five-story `llllX` flood table retains every source cell. It simplifies details including paired Genesis animals, Manu's generation of humanity, and varying southern Chinese traditions; later comparison changes to Yu, who is not the Chinese example in the table.
- Kuafu is described as catching the sun and later as pursuing what cannot be caught. Both source readings remain. Its literal forest name and subsequent peach-forest interpretation are also preserved separately.
- The source infers late emergence of a creation story from late surviving written attestation, without distinguishing oral antiquity from attestation date.
- The tablet chronology compares an excavated object with the Bible's composition without separating the age of a surviving copy from the age of a narrative tradition.
- Malinowski's wartime research is described as being stranded in the islands. This is the source's compressed biographical framing.
- Theoretical lenses are introduced as incompatible but later explicitly allowed to complement each other. Their stated limitations, especially the hypothetical status of Jung's collective unconscious, are retained.
- The Day of the Dead origin, consumer and technological analogies, and assertions about ancient literal belief are broad interpretations, not exhaustive histories or forecasts.

## Chapter 048 — Ritual

- The definition makes unexplained purpose necessary to ritual authority, despite subsequent examples with clearly articulated meanings. This source tension is retained.
- Ramadan is described without exemptions and people worldwide as waiting for the same sunset. The latter overlooks time zones; pilgrimage attendance and clothing descriptions likewise simplify variation and specify men only in the earlier passage.
- The penalty-kick example has a player touching goalposts three times; it remains the source's example rather than a verified general sporting practice.
- The quoted phrase about measuring feeling and establishing form contains five Chinese characters, while the source calls it four. The count is retained.
- The four-layer section treats supernatural claims as subject to public testing, broader than Chapter 046's categorical bracketing. Its rain-causation example is distinguished in practice from non-testable metaphysical claims, but the source does not fully resolve the methodological difference.
- The text generalises funeral duties and ancient coming-of-age customs from particular traditions. Liminality examples also compress distinctions among social rank, clothing, gender, and actual equality.
- Psychological effects are source generalisations, not promises of universal benefit. Its explicit cautions about compulsory participation, humiliation, manipulation, and theoretical limits remain intact.

## Chapter 049 — Judaism

- The coffin scene begins in summer 70 CE under Titus, then has Yohanan meet Vespasian and receive news of Nero's death and Vespasian's accession. These events belong to differing years; the source's explicit later-legend caveat is retained.
- The source names Talmud 56b's tractate “Gifts.” This is retained rather than silently replacing the title; the familiar Yohanan narrative is associated with Gittin, whose title concerns divorce documents.
- The account of Cyrus's cylinder generalises returns and temple restoration to Judeans without distinguishing what the cylinder itself names from biblical accounts.
- PaRDeS is introduced as a later summary, but its placement can suggest immediate post-70 origins; the translation preserves the later qualification. Avot 1:1 is also called an earliest Mishnah chapter without explaining textual chronology.
- Minyan is simplified as a congregation rather than a quorum, and community reproduction shifts from ten men to ten households. Literacy rates and universally synchronised annual readings are broad generalisations across diverse periods and communities.
- Orthodox observance of all 613 commandments is stated categorically despite commandments dependent on temple, office, or other conditions. Descriptions of Sabbath electricity and travel also simplify detailed practice.
- Kaifeng's history is framed as assimilation through welcome and no harm, omitting additional disruptions and ongoing identities. The source's anti-determinist counterexample is preserved without converting it into a complete history.
- Anderson's argument is simplified as requiring print, then extended to much older communities. The source does not distinguish his specific account of modern nationalism from every kind of imagined belonging.

## Chapter 050 — Christianity

- The fish-lamp recognition code and its possible appearance in Corinth in 56 CE are the source's illustrative reconstruction, not independently established first-century practice. The Last Supper is placed outside Jerusalem; this wording is retained.
- The statements that all other failed prophetic movements vanished and that historians can establish the disciples' willingness to die are broad claims retained with the source's distinction between belief and historical evidence.
- Peter and James are grouped against Paul on Gentile admission, simplifying the positions and chronology of the relevant narratives.
- The introduction dates all twenty-seven New Testament writings to the first century; some, notably 2 Peter, are commonly dated later. Its public-domain statement is retained; English quotations are explicitly identified as translations of the source's Chinese Union Version quotations.
- The Caesar passage is described as humanity's first such clear political boundary, a sweeping priority claim. Nicaea's formation of an empire-wide Christian boundary likewise compresses earlier and later developments.
- The Edict of Milan is attributed to Constantine alone, omitting Licinius and earlier toleration. The consequences of establishment in 380 are compressed into immediate universal exclusion from high office and temple closure.
- The church–sect framework is an ideal-type interpretation, not a universal sequence. The description of 1054 as a completed formal split compresses a long process.
- Pentecostal and charismatic Christianity are placed outside Catholicism and Orthodoxy, overlooking renewal movements within them. Regional membership figures are unsourced, and “southern hemisphere” substitutes geography for the broader Global South, including numerous northern-hemisphere examples.
- The Ethiopian church's name is retained as Ethiopian Orthodox; it belongs to the Oriental Orthodox communion, distinct from the Eastern Orthodox churches discussed earlier.
- Assertions that no church in Europe or America and no Christian outside China knows Christmas Eve apple-giving are categorical source generalisations. The ping/ping'an wordplay is explained in pinyin without altering the example.
