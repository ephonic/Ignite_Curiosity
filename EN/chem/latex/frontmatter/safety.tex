This book believes science is a hands-on subject. Before using your hands, however, read this page in full.

\section*{How the Book Classifies Experiments}

The Try It Yourself features use materials safely available at home or in an ordinary classroom: salt, sugar, vinegar, baking soda, red cabbage, milk, cooking oil, yeast, apples, potatoes, colored pens, straws, and similar items. These experiments can be done in a well-ventilated place with an adult aware of what you are doing.

The following are \textbf{for observation only, never for trying yourself}. The book describes them or recommends appropriate educational videos:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item Reactions of alkali metals (lithium, sodium, potassium) with water or acids;
\item Concentrated acids, concentrated alkalis, chlorine, or mixing bleach with other cleaners;
\item Radioactive materials and high-energy radiation;
\item Extraction or distillation with organic solvents, and any flammable vapors or open flames;
\item Culturing bacteria, antibiotic experiments, or any genetic-modification procedures;
\item Human samples, microorganisms of unknown origin, or medical testing;
\item Intense ultraviolet light, high-voltage electrolysis, or high-temperature and high-pressure reactions.
\end{itemize}

\section*{General Rules}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item Before any experiment, read the entire feature and confirm the materials, procedure, and disposal method.
\item Tell an adult at home or school what you are doing. An adult must perform or continuously supervise any heating.
\item Never taste experimental materials. Even food, once used in an experiment, must not return to the table.
\item Keep experimental equipment separate from eating utensils and wash it afterward. Dispose of liquids according to the feature's instructions; liquids from this book's experiments can generally be flushed down the drain with plenty of water.
\item Do not make ``more exciting'' substitutions. If the instructions say vinegar, do not use toilet cleaner. If they say baking soda, do not use an unidentified white powder.
\item If an accident occurs---a spill, a splash on skin or eyes, or accidental ingestion---stop immediately, rinse with plenty of clean water, and tell an adult at once.
\end{enumerate}

The greatest experimenters in scientific history understood how to control risk. Caution is professionalism, not timidity.
